Meet Your Professor

Welcome to the course

Hello everyone

My name is Professor Fomba Kassoh, and I will be your instructor for this course.

My academic and professional background spans Data Science, Artificial Intelligence, Business Analytics, Civil Engineering, Transportation, and Water Resources. In addition to teaching at Monroe University, I work in the transportation industry, where I manage technology and operational projects involving data, automated systems, and decision-making.

How we will learn

My goal is to help you move beyond memorizing AI terminology. We will use examples, visual explanations, algorithm traces, Python code, and interactive participation activities to understand how intelligent systems solve problems.

You will be expected to explain not only what an algorithm does, but why it behaves that way, when it should be used, and what tradeoffs it makes.

Course Overview

What CS650 is about

CS650 develops a practical and conceptual understanding of artificial intelligence. Students learn how intelligent agents represent problems, explore alternatives, use search algorithms, apply heuristics, and reason about the quality and efficiency of solutions.

Current Learning Objectives

What you should be able to do

  • Formulate a task as a search problem using states, actions, a transition model, goal test, and action cost.
  • Explain the distinction between a state space, search tree, frontier, and reached set.
  • Trace breadth-first, depth-first, and uniform-cost search step by step.
  • Compare search strategies using completeness, cost optimality, time complexity, and space complexity.
  • Explain the difference between uninformed and informed search.
  • Explain how greedy best-first search uses a heuristic.
  • Explain how A* combines path cost g(n) with heuristic cost h(n).
  • Explain admissibility, consistency, and why heuristic quality matters.
  • Connect classical search techniques to routing, robotics, puzzles, scheduling, and other AI applications.
